The Watts Family Murders: What the Record Shows

November 14, 2025  ·  Updated September 14, 2026  ·  Cory

On August 13, 2018, a pregnant mother and her two young daughters vanished from a five-bedroom home in Frederick, Colorado. The strangest true detail of this case is how quickly the husband's public performance fell apart under the weight of his own security camera footage and digital footprint.

Christopher Lee Watts stood in front of television cameras, pleading for the safe return of 34-year-old Shanann Cathryn Rzucek, 4-year-old Bella Marie Watts, and 3-year-old Celeste Cathryn Watts. He gave an emotional interview to Denver stations KMGH-TV and KUSA-TV. But while he spoke, investigators with search dogs were already working on the property.

You have probably heard the headline version of this case. The media heavily covered the psychological profile of the killer and the tragic loss of Shanann, who was 15 weeks pregnant with a boy to be named Nico. Let's unpack it. Here is what the case file actually shows.

The Disappearance and the Welfare Check

At approximately 1:48 a.m. on Monday, August 13, 2018, Shanann returned from a business trip to Arizona. Her friend and colleague, Nickole Utoft Atkinson, dropped her off and watched her walk into the house at 2825 Saratoga Trail. Over the weekend, Watts had been at home with Bella and Celeste. Later that morning, Shanann failed to attend a scheduled obstetrics and gynecology appointment and did not respond to text messages.

When Shanann also missed a business meeting, Atkinson went to the Watts residence at about 12:10 p.m. After repeated knocks and doorbell rings went unanswered, she contacted Watts at his job with Anadarko Petroleum and notified the Frederick Police Department. A police officer arrived at approximately 1:40 p.m. to conduct a welfare check. Watts would not allow the officer permission to search the home until he arrived.

Inside, the family dog was found unharmed, but there was no trace of Shanann or the children. Searchers located Shanann's purse, which contained her keys and medication. Her phone was discovered between the couch cushions in the living room. Her car, with the girls' car seats still inside, remained parked in the garage, and her wedding ring was found on a bedside table.

The Investigation and the Camera Footage

The FBI and the Colorado Bureau of Investigation joined the search on August 14. Watts initially told police that he did not know where his family might be. He claimed he had last seen his wife at 5:15 a.m. on August 13 when he left for work. However, the timeline did not match the evidence.

On November 29, 2018, Colorado authorities released surveillance footage recorded in the early morning hours of August 13 by a neighbor's security camera. The video showed Watts backing his pickup truck into his garage at 5:17 a.m. He was seen loading a gas can into the bed of the truck and walking back and forth several times carrying bundles.

The footage demonstrated that Shanann did not leave the house with the children that morning, contradicting Watts's initial statements to police. Investigators concluded that he was transporting his daughters and his wife's body to a remote oil site. Police also recovered phone data and text messages that confirmed Watts was having an extra-marital affair with a female co-worker named Nichol Kessinger.

The Confessions and the Recoveries

On August 15, Watts was administered a polygraph examination, which he failed. Prompted by the presence of his father, Ronnie, Watts provided an initial confession to investigators. He admitted that he killed Shanann, but he falsely claimed that he did so in a rage only after witnessing her smother Bella and Celeste.

Watts marked the locations of the bodies on a photograph of the Anadarko Petroleum oil site for investigators. They arrived at the site at 11:00 p.m. Shanann was found buried in a shallow grave nearby. The bodies of Bella and Celeste were recovered from separate crude-oil storage tanks after the tanks were drained. Each child's body had been pushed through a hatch at the top of a different tank, with an opening measuring only 8 inches in diameter.

The medical examiner noted that Bella had scratches on her left buttock from being shoved through the hole, and a tuft of blonde hair was found on the edge of one of the hatches. Watts was arrested that day, and his employment at Anadarko was terminated. He later acknowledged that he had murdered his daughters as well, smothering them with his bare hands.

Financial Strain and the Affair

The background of the Watts marriage showed signs of financial and emotional strain. The couple purchased their five-bedroom home in 2013 but filed for bankruptcy in 2015, reportedly nearly 450,000 dollars in debt. Shanann worked from home selling a product called Thrive for the multi-level marketing company Le-Vel, while Watts worked as a contractor.

In mid-2018, Watts began an extra-marital affair with Kessinger. During a six-week period when Shanann took the girls to visit her parents in North Carolina, Watts vacationed with Kessinger and told her he was separated and in the process of divorcing Shanann. By July, text messages showed Shanann questioning her husband's distant behavior.

In a recorded interview released by the CBI, Kessinger described changes in Watts's behavior in the days leading up to the murders. After seeing Watts on the news and realizing he lied about the state of his marriage and Shanann's pregnancy, Kessinger told police about the affair two days after Shanann was reported missing.

Changing Stories and Premeditation

Watts's account of the murders changed multiple times. In his initial confession, he claimed he acted in a sudden rage after an argument about divorce. In an interview on Dr. Phil, his attorney stated that during the attack, Bella entered the room, and Watts told her that Shanann was ill before loading them into his work truck.

In June 2021, Inside Edition reported additional confessions Watts made in letters to a pen pal named Cheryln Cadle. In these letters, Watts stated that he had planned the murders for several weeks. He claimed that the oxycodone found in Shanann's system had been administered by him in an attempt to end her pregnancy, believing it would make it easier to remain with his girlfriend.

Watts also wrote that he had attempted to smother his daughters in their beds at home before killing Shanann, but they survived the initial attack. He then took them to the work site where they were ultimately killed.

The Plea Deal and Sentencing

On August 21, prosecutors charged Watts with multiple counts, including three counts of first-degree murder, unlawful termination of a pregnancy, and three counts of tampering with a deceased human body. The murder charges included additional counts for each child under a provision covering the death of a child under 12 by a person in a position of trust.

On November 6, 2018, Watts pleaded guilty to the murders. The District Attorney of Weld County did not pursue the death penalty at the request of Shanann's family, who stated they did not wish for any further deaths. On November 19, Watts was sentenced to five life terms without the possibility of parole, three of them to be served consecutively. He received an additional 48 years for the unlawful termination of Shanann's pregnancy and 36 years for tampering with a deceased human body.

Due to security concerns, Watts was transferred out of state. On December 5, 2018, he arrived at Dodge Correctional Institution, a maximum-security prison in Waupun, Wisconsin, where he continues to serve his life sentences.

The Aftermath for the Family

Following the murders, Shanann's family faced years of relentless online harassment. In a documentary, Shanann's father, Frank Rzucek, described the vitriol fueled by true crime conspiracy theories. He stated, I never saw so much evil in this world. Towards us, towards other victims.

The family became advocates for domestic violence prevention. In 2019, they won a 6 million dollar civil lawsuit against Watts, though his imprisonment means they are unlikely to receive the judgment. In 2025, Shanann's brother, Frankie Rzucek, won a defamation and harassment lawsuit in the United Kingdom against a YouTuber accused of spreading false claims, resulting in the channel's removal.

The Watts case remains one of the most gripping examples of family annihilation in recent history. I went back through the affidavit and the timeline to lay out exactly what happened. Read the file. Form your own conclusion.

The Courtney Clenney Case: What the Evidence Shows

October 2, 2025  ·  Updated September 14, 2026  ·  Cory

The strangest true detail of this case was established on day one. When Courtney Clenney called 911 on April 3, 2022, she admitted to killing her boyfriend, Christian Obumseli. She told dispatchers they had a violent fight. She claimed he threw her to the ground. She said she grabbed a knife and threw it at him from about ten feet away.

Then the medical examiner looked at the body. The autopsy report showed Obumseli died from a forceful downward thrust. The blade went three inches into his chest. It pierced a major artery. The medical examiner concluded the fatal wound could not have been caused by a knife thrown from that distance.

Clenney is a former OnlyFans and Instagram model who was known online as Courtney Tailor. For years, she faced a second-degree murder charge while her defense team argued she acted in self-defense. You have probably heard the headline version of this story. Let's unpack what the evidence actually shows.

The April 2022 Stabbing

Christian Obumseli was 27 years old. He worked as a cryptocurrency investor. He shared a Miami, Florida apartment with Clenney. On the day of his death, police responded to that condominium and found him suffering from a severe stab wound. He later died from his injuries.

The medical examiner determined that the knife severed his subclavian artery. The physical evidence immediately contradicted Clenney's initial claim about throwing the weapon from across the room.

Miami-Dade State Attorney Katherine Fernandez Rundle characterized the fatal event as the culmination of a tempestuous and combative relationship. Investigators began looking backward. They wanted to know exactly what happened between the couple before that final day in April.

A History of Violence

The relationship began in November 2020. Before moving to Florida, the couple lived in a penthouse at the Berkshire Riverview in South Austin, Texas. A former neighbor named Aidan Nesvisky lived directly below them.

Nesvisky told reporters that fights between Clenney and Obumseli were frequent during the two years they lived there. He said the disputes got so bad they literally spilled over into his space. During one violent argument, a tiger painting was thrown down onto his tenth-floor balcony.

Police were called to the couple's Austin apartment multiple times. When they relocated to Miami, the violence continued. State Attorney Rundle noted that things escalated to the point where their building management was prepared to evict them. A video recorded in February 2022 during a trip to Aspen, Colorado, also surfaced. It appeared to show Clenney yelling at and repeatedly hitting Obumseli in front of a person who filmed the encounter.

The Self-Defense Claim

In August 2022, authorities arrested Clenney in Hawaii. She was at a rehabilitation facility receiving treatment for substance abuse and post-traumatic stress disorder. She was extradited to Florida and held without bond.

Her attorney, Frank Prieto, argued that the stabbing was self-defense. The defense pushed for the case to be considered manslaughter rather than second-degree murder. They claimed Obumseli repeatedly engaged in a pattern of physical violence, intimidation, and emotional abuse.

The defense filed court documents outlining text messages to corroborate her account. In an April 2021 exchange, Clenney accused Obumseli of hitting her in the face. Obumseli allegedly replied that he was trying to move her hand and did not mean to hit her. In August 2021, she texted that he had been punching her in the back.

Her legal team also detailed other alleged incidents. Clenney claimed Obumseli dislocated her shoulder during a September 2021 argument over leaving a pool. She alleged he fractured her ribs during a 2021 trip to Dubai after an argument about smoking marijuana. She also claimed an incident in Austin in January 2022 left her with an injured finger.

The Laptop and the Parents

While prosecutors built their murder case, a separate investigation opened into Clenney's parents. Dewayne and Deborah Lyn Clenney live in Bee Cave, Texas. Investigators believed Clenney gave Obumseli's laptop to her parents sometime between the killing and her arrest.

The parents were arrested in Austin for tampering with evidence. They were charged with one count of unauthorized access to a computer, which is a third-degree felony in Florida. An attorney for Obumseli's estate confirmed they never gave anyone permission to keep or access his laptop.

Warrants for iCloud accounts uncovered a group chat between Clenney's parents and her attorneys. In one message, Clenney's father, referred to as Kim in the court documents, allegedly texted, "Hell yeah! That PIN worked." An attorney replied, "Kim. Hold off going through the computer please. I don't want to turn you into a witness just yet if you find something useful." Deborah Clenney allegedly transported the laptop to a Dallas attorney before it was given to an IT expert in Florida.

In July 2024, South Florida prosecutors dropped the computer hacking charges. A circuit judge ruled the Miami-Dade State Attorney's Office violated attorney-client privilege when they confiscated those texts and emails. Defense attorney Jude Faccidomo called for prosecutors to recuse themselves from the murder case entirely.

The Civil Suit and Pretrial Delays

As the criminal case dragged on, Obumseli's family filed a civil lawsuit. They sued Clenney for battery. They also sued several companies connected to the couple's apartment building, alleging the condominium, property management, and security defendants failed to provide adequate security.

Court records show Clenney resolved the civil claims well before her criminal case concluded. On December 3, 2025, the estate filed a notice of settlement for Clenney only. The claims against her were voluntarily dismissed with prejudice on March 4, 2026.

Meanwhile, the criminal trial faced constant delays. Both sides litigated issues involving evidence and pretrial motions. Judge Andrea Ricker Wolfson expressed frustration at an April 2026 hearing regarding subpoenaed witnesses failing to appear for depositions. The judge made it clear the trial would not be delayed forever. A new trial date was set for April 27, 2026.

The Plea Deal

The case never made it to a jury. Days before the trial was expected to finally begin, attorney Frank Prieto told reporters that Clenney had reached a deal. On August 10, 2026, Clenney appeared inside a Miami courtroom and accepted a plea agreement.

She pleaded guilty to first-degree manslaughter with a deadly weapon. The judge sentenced her to six years in prison, followed by five years of probation. Clenney received credit for the nearly four years she had already spent behind bars since her 2022 arrest.

The agreement requires her to undergo mental health and substance abuse evaluations upon release. Assistant State Attorney Shawn Abuhoff also stated that prosecutors would support efforts by the Florida Attorney General's Office to prevent Clenney from profiting financially from the killing.

The Final Word in Court

At the sentencing hearing, Christian's family addressed the court. They described him as someone who loved deeply. They acknowledged he remained in a destructive relationship despite previous violence. The family noted he had written about fears that his love for Clenney could ultimately cost him his life.

His mother, Chio Obumseli, delivered a victim impact statement. She said her son was kindhearted, compassionate, friendly, and respectful. "Four years later, there are still nights when I cannot sleep," she told the court. "Nothing can describe what it feels like for a mother to stand at the grave of her child."

The family representative told Clenney her actions left a void that can never be filled. The legal process is over, and the sentence is handed down. Read the file. Form your own conclusion.

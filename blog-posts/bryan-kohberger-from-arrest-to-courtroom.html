The 2022 University of Idaho Student Murders

September 20, 2025  ·  Updated September 14, 2026  ·  Cory

You have probably heard the headline version of this case. A quiet college town. A house full of students. A brutal crime in the middle of the night. But when you look past the noise and the internet rumors, the strangest true detail of the case is a single item left behind on a bed: a tan leather knife sheath holding a microscopic trace of DNA. That one piece of evidence cracked open a massive investigation.

On November 13, 2022, 4 University of Idaho students were fatally stabbed in an off-campus house in Moscow, Idaho. The town of Moscow, with a population of 25,000, had not recorded a single murder since 2015. The victims were Madison Mogen, Kaylee Goncalves, Ethan Chapin, and Xana Kernodle. The brutality of the crime sent shockwaves through the community, prompting an early Thanksgiving exodus and launching a manhunt that gripped the entire country.

I went back through the case file to see exactly how investigators put the pieces together. The record points to Bryan Christopher Kohberger, a 28-year-old criminology PhD student at neighboring Washington State University. In 2025, Kohberger pled guilty to 4 counts of first-degree murder and 1 count of felony burglary. Let's unpack what the evidence actually shows.

The House on King Road

During the fall 2022 semester, 5 female University of Idaho students rented a 3-story off-campus residence at 1122 King Road. Built on a hillside, the house had entrances on both the 1st and 2nd floors. It had 6 bedrooms, with 2 on each floor. Madison Mogen and Kaylee Goncalves occupied the 3rd floor. Xana Kernodle and surviving roommate Dylan Mortensen lived on the 2nd floor. The 1st floor held the bedroom of Bethany Funke, the other surviving roommate.

The victims were deeply embedded in the campus community. Madison Mogen, 21, and Kaylee Goncalves, 21, grew up in Coeur d'Alene, Idaho, and had been best friends since childhood. Mogen was a senior majoring in marketing, a member of the Pi Beta Phi sorority, and worked at the Mad Greek restaurant. Goncalves was a senior majoring in general studies and a member of the Alpha Phi sorority. She was set to graduate early the following month and had a job secured with an IT firm in Texas.

Xana Kernodle, 20, was a junior majoring in marketing. Like Mogen, she was in the Pi Beta Phi sorority and worked at the Mad Greek restaurant. Her boyfriend, Ethan Chapin, 20, was staying in her room on the night of the murders. Chapin was a sophomore majoring in recreation, sport, and tourism management, and a member of the Sigma Chi fraternity.

By 2:00 a.m. on November 13, 2022, all 5 roommates and Chapin were inside the house. Chapin and Kernodle returned from a Sigma Chi party at 1:45 a.m. Mogen and Goncalves returned at 1:56 a.m. after visiting the Corner Club sports bar and the Grub Truck food stand. Between 2:26 and 2:52 a.m., the phones of Goncalves and Mogen made 7 unanswered calls to a former boyfriend of Goncalves.

The Timeline of the Attack

Detectives believe the 4 murders occurred between 4:00 and 4:25 a.m. Kernodle appears to have been awake. She received a DoorDash food order around 4:00 a.m. and was using the TikTok app on her phone at 4:12 a.m. On the 2nd floor, Dylan Mortensen woke up to a noise on the 3rd floor. She initially thought it was Goncalves playing with her dog. Mortensen later heard someone say, "There's someone here." Investigators believe this voice may have been Kernodle.

Mortensen looked out her door but saw nothing. She then heard crying from Kernodle's room. She opened her door a 2nd time and heard an unfamiliar male voice say, "It's okay; I'm going to help you." At 4:17 a.m., a security camera less than 50 feet from Kernodle's room recorded a dog barking and "distorted audio of what sounded like voices or a whimper followed by a loud thud."

Mortensen opened her door a 3rd time. She saw a figure clad in black clothing and a mask covering his mouth and nose. She described him as at least 5-foot-10, athletically built, with bushy eyebrows. The masked stranger walked past her and exited through a sliding glass door in the kitchen. Mortensen stood in a "frozen shock phase" before locking herself in her room.

Between 4:22 and 4:24 a.m., the 2 surviving roommates texted each other. Mortensen texted Funke, "I'm rlly confused rn," and then, "I'm freaking out rn." Mortensen also texted Goncalves, "What's going on." Mortensen mentioned seeing someone in "like a ski mask almost" to Funke, who replied, "Stfu."

The Discovery and the Autopsies

At approximately 4:26 a.m., Mortensen went downstairs to join Funke. On the way, she saw Kernodle lying on the floor but believed she might be intoxicated. The 2 roommates locked themselves in Funke's bedroom for several hours. The following morning, they called friends over for help. A friend went to the 2nd floor, found that Chapin and Kernodle had no pulses, and told everyone to exit. Funke called 911 at 11:55:42 a.m. to report an "unconscious person."

All 4 victims were pronounced dead at the scene that afternoon. Mogen and Goncalves were found in a single bed on the 3rd floor. Kernodle was on the floor of her 2nd-floor bedroom, and Chapin was in the bed. All 4 were stabbed to death with a single-edged, non-serrated knife. The victims were not gagged or restrained, and none were sexually assaulted. Goncalves' dog was found unharmed on the 3rd floor.

Unsealed autopsy reports by Dr. Veena Singh revealed the victims suffered at least 150 stab wounds combined. Kernodle sustained the most catastrophic injuries, with at least 67 wounds. Many were defensive, suggesting she fought back. Forensic death investigator Joseph Scott Morgan called the attack on Kernodle "overkill" and a "frenzied event."

Goncalves sustained approximately 38 sharp-force injuries. She also suffered blunt-force injuries to her face, a broken nose, and signs of asphyxia. Mogen sustained at least 28 stab wounds. Investigators found no blood on the bottoms of Mogen's feet, indicating she did not stand during the attack. Chapin sustained about 17 sharp-force wounds, including a fatal stab under his left clavicle.

The Digital Trail

Nearly 130 law enforcement personnel worked the case, receiving over 15,000 tips by December 24. A major break came from surveillance cameras. Footage captured a white 2011 to 2013 Hyundai Elantra making multiple passes near the residence. The car passed the home 3 times beginning around 3:29 a.m. It returned for a 4th time at 4:04 a.m. At 4:20 a.m., the car was seen speeding away from the neighborhood.

Investigators in Pullman, Washington, began looking at a white Elantra owned by Bryan Christopher Kohberger. Kohberger was a 28-year-old PhD student in criminology at Washington State University, located less than 8 miles from Moscow. On November 29, a campus police officer found the vehicle registered to Kohberger.

Cell phone data placed Kohberger on the radar. Records showed his phone stopped connecting to the network at 2:47 a.m. on November 13 and reconnected at 4:48 a.m. south of Moscow. The data also showed his phone connected to a cell tower near the victims' residence around 9:00 a.m., roughly 5 hours after the murders. Furthermore, his phone had pinged near the residence at least 12 times between June 2022 and November 13.

In December, Kohberger drove across the country to Pennsylvania with his father in the white Elantra. On December 15, they were pulled over 2 times within a 5-mile radius in Indiana for traffic violations. Meanwhile, Washington State University terminated Kohberger from his teaching assistant role on December 19 due to behavior and performance issues.

The Knife Sheath and the Arrest

The physical evidence tying the case together was found on Mogen's bed: a tan leather knife sheath. Investigators extracted touch DNA from the sheath. It yielded a single male profile with no matches in the national CODIS database. On November 22, investigators gave a sample to forensic genealogy company Othram, which found a match sharing 70.7 centimorgans of DNA.

The FBI took over the genetic genealogy on December 10. Using GEDmatch and MyHeritage, the FBI identified a match with 250 shared centimorgans, pointing directly to Kohberger's family tree. Investigators began monitoring Kohberger at his parents' home in Albrightsville, Pennsylvania.

Agents observed Kohberger wearing surgical gloves and placing trash bags into a neighbor's garbage can. Authorities stated he "cleaned his car, inside and outside, not missing an inch". On December 27, police recovered trash from the Pennsylvania home. A lab determined the DNA belonged to the father of the male who left DNA on the knife sheath.

On December 30, 2022, the Pennsylvania State Police and the FBI executed an arrest warrant. They found Kohberger in the kitchen, wearing examination gloves and putting trash into separate zip-lock bags. A search of the home revealed a knife, a pistol, a black face mask, and ID cards inside a glove in a box. Kohberger waived extradition and was flown to Idaho.

The Court Record

In May 2023, a grand jury indicted Kohberger on 4 counts of first-degree murder and 1 count of felony burglary. Prosecutors initially sought the death penalty. The defense filed numerous pretrial motions, and on September 9, 2024, the Idaho Supreme Court granted a change of venue to Ada County District Court in Boise.

On July 2, 2025, Kohberger entered a plea agreement. He pled guilty to all 5 counts to avoid the death penalty and waived his right to appeal. On July 23, 2025, Judge Steven Hippler sentenced Kohberger to 4 consecutive life sentences without the possibility of parole, plus 10 years for the burglary conviction. Kohberger was ordered to pay a $250,000 fine and $20,000 in restitution.

During the sentencing hearing, family members delivered impact statements. Kohberger offered no formal statement. However, in July 2026, Kohberger filed a petition for post-conviction relief. He claimed he was innocent and that his former defense team coerced him into the plea deal through misinformation. On August 29, 2026, he asked the court to disqualify Judge Hippler.

The families of the victims maintain their confidence in the conviction. The house at 1122 King Road was demolished, and the University of Idaho opened the Vandal Healing Garden in 2024 to honor the students. The case file is public. Read the file. Form your own conclusion.

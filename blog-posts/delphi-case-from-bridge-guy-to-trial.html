The Delphi Murders: What the Record Shows

February 10, 2025  ·  Updated September 14, 2026  ·  Cory

You've probably heard the headline version of the Delphi murders. Two teenagers vanished while hiking, and one of them managed to capture video and audio of the suspect on her phone before she died. For five years, the case file sat open while the town of Delphi, Indiana, waited for answers.

The strangest true detail of the case, stated plainly: the man eventually convicted of the murders had placed himself at the scene just three days after the crime, but a filing error kept his name out of the active investigation for over five years.

I went back through the affidavit, the trial testimony, and the case file to see what the evidence actually shows. Let's unpack it. Here's a look at the documented facts of the Delphi murders, the investigation, and where the case stands today.

The Disappearance on the Monon High Bridge

On February 13, 2017, 13-year-old Abigail "Abby" Joyce Williams and 14-year-old Liberty Rose "Libby" Lynn German were dropped off by German's older sister, Kelsi German. It was 1:30 p.m. The girls were dropped off on County Road 300 North, east of the Hoosier Heartland Highway, to hike on the Monon High Bridge over Deer Creek.

At 2:07 p.m., German posted a photo of Williams walking on the bridge to social media. After that post, the girls were not heard from again. They failed to meet German's father at 3:15 p.m., and were reported missing at 5:30 p.m.

The families initially searched for the girls themselves before calling the police. Authorities searching the area did not initially suspect foul play. That changed around noon the next day, when the bodies of the girls were found on the north bank of Deer Creek, about 0.5 miles east of the abandoned Monon High Bridge.

The Evidence Left Behind

Officials credited German's smartphone for securing crucial evidence. On February 15, 2017, the Indiana State Police began circulating a still image of an individual walking on the rail bridge toward the girls. The person, dubbed the "Bridge Guy," was named the prime suspect.

At a press conference on February 22, law enforcement released an audio recording from the phone. The muffled voice of the suspect is heard saying, "Down the hill." Police indicated that additional evidence from the phone had been secured but withheld further details to protect future trial proceedings.

Investigators also recovered an unspent .40-caliber round less than two feet from one victim's body, between the two victims. The forensic digital examiner, First Lieutenant Christopher Cecil, later testified that German's phone recorded a failed attempt to open it using a fingerprint at 2.14pm, just one minute after a video was taken at 2.13pm. The phone stopped moving for the last time at 2.32pm.

The Misfiled Tip

In September 2022, a volunteer file clerk named Kathy Shank was reviewing tips when she discovered a misfiled document. The file was marked "cleared," but Shank noticed an error. The tip, submitted three days after the murders, was from a "Richard Allen Whiteman" who self-reported being on the trails between 1:30 p.m. and 3:30 p.m. on the day of the crime.

Shank deduced that the tip was regarding Richard Allen, who lived on Whiteman Drive, and that the names had been transposed. She brought the file to investigators. In the tip, Allen stated he had seen three girls on the trails that day.

On October 26, 2022, Richard Allen was taken into custody. On October 31, the Indiana State Police announced that Allen had been charged with two counts of murder. He pled not guilty.

The Case Against Richard Allen

According to the probable cause affidavit unsealed by Judge Frances Gull on November 29, 2022, investigators believed Allen was the man seen in the video on German's phone. The affidavit noted that the unspent .40-caliber round found between the victims was later determined to have come from a gun owned by Allen.

The affidavit also detailed witness accounts. One witness reported seeing a man walking away from the bridge "wearing a blue colored jacket and blue jeans and was muddy and bloody." Another witness described a car parked "oddly" as if to hide its license plate, which investigators said matched a vehicle Allen owned in 2017.

During a 2022 interview, Allen told authorities he had worn "jeans and a black or blue jacket" on the day of the murders and had gone to the bridge to "watch fish."

The Trial and Testimony

The trial of Richard Allen began on October 18, 2024, in Delphi. Prosecutors stated both girls had their throats cut. Williams was found fully clothed in German's clothing, whereas German was discovered nude.

Forensic pathologist Dr. Roland Kohr testified about the autopsies, noting that both girls bled to death. Neither showed physical signs of sexual trauma or defensive wounds, though German's hands were covered in blood. A police firearms examiner testified that the unspent .40 caliber bullet was linked to Allen's gun through the "quality and quantity of marks."

Prosecutors also presented evidence of Allen's confessions. They stated that Allen had admitted to the murders more than 60 times while incarcerated, confessing to his wife, his mother, family members, the prison warden, a psychologist, and other inmates. A State Police Master Trooper testified that he had listened to more than 700 of Allen's prison phone calls and concluded that "the voice of the 'Bridge Guy' is the voice of Richard Allen."

The Defense and Verdict

The defense argued that Allen's mental health had deteriorated rapidly during his 13 months in solitary confinement, suggesting he was in a psychotic state during his confessions. A clinical psychologist testified for the defense that Allen was "diagnosed with a serious mental illness" and had a "grave disability."

The psychologist also testified that Allen told her he had "originally planned to sexually assault the victims but ran away when he saw a van nearby, and he had cut the girls' throats and covered their bodies with sticks." A neuropsychologist hired by the defense testified that Allen suffered from "pretty severe depression" and anxiety.

On November 11, 2024, the sequestered jury convicted Allen on all counts. On December 20, 2024, Judge Gull sentenced him to 65 years for the murder of German and 65 years for the murder of Williams, to be served consecutively, resulting in a total of 130 years in prison.

Where It Stands

In April 2025, videos of Allen's interrogation and prison confessions were released to the public. Allen was later transferred out of Indiana to the Lexington Assessment and Reception Center in Oklahoma under an interstate compact agreement.

The Delphi community continues to remember Abigail Williams and Liberty German. The L & A Park Foundation developed Abby and Libby Memorial Park, and the Delphi Community Middle School renamed its library the "Abby and Libby Memorial Library."

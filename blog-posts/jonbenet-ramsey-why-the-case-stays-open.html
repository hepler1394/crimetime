Why the JonBenét Ramsey Case Is Still Open After Nearly 30 Years

January 8, 2026  ·  Updated October 5, 2026  ·  Cory

The Boulder police file on JonBenét Ramsey holds nearly 2,500 pieces of evidence and about 40,000 reports, more than a million pages in all. Detectives have collected samples from more than 200 people. Nearly 30 years after the six-year-old was found dead in the basement of her family's home on December 26, 1996, no one has been charged.

The crime itself and the first decade of the investigation are covered in a separate post. This one is about why the case has not closed: a DNA profile that has never been matched to anyone, a grand jury vote that never became a prosecution, and what Boulder police say they are doing now.

The Unknown DNA

Early in the investigation, analysts found male DNA in a drop of blood on JonBenét's underwear. It did not come from anyone in her family. In 2003 a profile from that sample was entered into CODIS, the FBI's national DNA database. It has never produced a match.

In 2008, the office of Boulder County District Attorney Mary Lacy sent JonBenét's long underwear to Bode Technology, a private lab near Washington, D.C., for "touch DNA" testing, which looks for skin cells left by contact. The lab found previously undetected male genetic material on the sides of the garment, where an attacker would have grasped it, and it was consistent with the profile from the underwear.

On July 9, 2008, Lacy announced that the Ramsey family was no longer under suspicion. In a letter to John Ramsey she wrote: "To the extent that we may have contributed in any way to the public perception that you might have been involved in this crime, I am deeply sorry." Patsy Ramsey had died of cancer two years earlier.

The letter has been disputed since. In 2016 Lacy's successor, Stan Garnett, said: "This letter is not legally binding. It's a good-faith opinion and has no legal importance." The same year the Boulder Daily Camera reported that independent DNA experts who reviewed the lab data believed the profile might be a composite of more than one person, which would limit its value for identifying anyone. Former police chief Mark Beckner, who did not agree with the exoneration, has said the unknown DNA should still be the focus of the investigation.

The sample is also small. In November 2022 Boulder police said the DNA available is "extremely small and complex" and that the sample "could, in whole or in part, be consumed by DNA testing." A test that uses up the evidence cannot be run again. The department has said that whenever a proven technology can reliably test samples like these, more analysis will be done, and that it has discussed genetic genealogy with private labs alongside the Colorado Bureau of Investigation and the FBI.

The Grand Jury

A Boulder County grand jury began hearing evidence on September 15, 1998, and sat for 13 months. On October 13, 1999, District Attorney Alex Hunter announced there would be no charges. "I and my prosecutorial team believe we do not have sufficient evidence to warrant the filing of charges against anyone who has been investigated at this time," he said.

He did not disclose that the jurors had voted to indict. The Boulder Daily Camera reported the vote earlier in 2013, and on October 25, 2013, after its reporter went to court, a judge released the four pages signed by the grand jury foreman. They listed two counts against each parent. The first, child abuse resulting in death, alleged that John and Patsy Ramsey permitted JonBenét to be "unreasonably placed in a situation which posed a threat of injury to the child's life or health." The second, accessory to a crime, alleged that each had helped a person suspected of first-degree murder and child abuse resulting in death. The pages did not identify that person. Because Hunter never signed the indictment, no charge was filed.

A grand jury indictment requires only probable cause. A conviction requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt. Garnett said in 2013 that the documents meant the jurors found probable cause on the evidence they heard, that when he had the proposed charges reviewed he "was told that they related to charges for which the statute of limitations had run years ago," and that "everyone, including the Ramsey family, is entitled to the full presumption of innocence." The family's lawyer, L. Lin Wood, called the released pages "a mere snippet of the case" that did not account for the 2008 DNA testing.

Burke Ramsey

JonBenét's brother, Burke, was nine. In 1999 both the police and the district attorney's office said publicly that he was not a suspect. A 2016 CBS documentary series nonetheless put forward the theory that he had killed his sister. He sued CBS and others for defamation, seeking $750 million, and on January 4, 2019, his lawyer said the case had "been amicably settled to the satisfaction of all parties."

Where the Case Stands, October 2026

Boulder police took the case back from the district attorney in February 2009 and have treated it as an open homicide since. Detectives have followed up on more than 21,000 tips, letters and emails and traveled to 19 states to interview or speak with more than 1,000 people. In 2023 the Colorado Cold Case Review Team spent a year building a searchable database of the file. Its recommendations have not been made public.

Stephen Redfearn, named police chief in September 2024, issues an update near each anniversary. On December 12, 2025, he said: "This past year, our detectives have conducted several new interviews as well as re-interviewed individuals based on tips we've received. We have also collected new evidence and tested and re-tested other pieces of evidence to generate new leads." The department has not said what the new evidence is or who was interviewed.

John Ramsey has pressed police since at least 2024 to send evidence to a private lab for forensic genetic genealogy, the method that has identified suspects in other cold cases by tracing a DNA profile through relatives. In June 2026 Yvonne "Missy" Woods, a former Colorado Bureau of Investigation DNA analyst, pleaded guilty to four felonies tied to her lab work, which raised new questions about the case. Boulder police said a CBI review of the records found Woods's conduct had no effect on the Ramsey investigation, and the district attorney's office said the same.

The department's position is that nothing is being left untested. Its case page says: "The assertion that there is viable evidence and leads we are not pursuing—to include DNA testing—is completely false."

December 26, 2026, will be 30 years. As of early October 2026, no arrest has been made and no one has been charged. Boulder police ask anyone with information to call 303-441-1974 or email BouldersMostWanted@bouldercolorado.gov.
